I lead Search at Zalando in Berlin: an organization of engineers and applied scientists working on retrieval, ranking, and LLM-based personalization. Before that, I led applied science for ranking, recommendations, and generative AI at Zalando.

Most of my career has been spent turning messy questions into systems that ship. At Amazon, I led applied-science teams behind customer reviews, star ratings, questions and answers, community moderation, and gift recommendations. Earlier, at souq.com, I worked on search, recommendations, and the data infrastructure underneath them.

## What this blog is for

Somewhere along the way I noticed that the hardest problems in production were rarely about the model. They were about definitions: what counts as a gift, a relevant result, an improvement, an understanding. That is where philosophy turns out to be useful. Not as decoration, but as a debugging tool for the assumptions we ship along with the code.

So this blog sits where those things meet:

- **Philosophy for builders.** Saussure, Wittgenstein, Hume, and Baudrillard as lenses on machine learning and the tech industry.
- **Decision-making under uncertainty.** Bandits, ranking, experiments, and how teams choose what to work on.
- **Leadership.** Short essays on running teams of scientists and engineers.
- **Interactive demos.** Models that actually train in your browser, so you can poke the idea instead of trusting my description of it.
- **The occasional story,** when an argument works better as fiction.

I write in English and in Levantine Arabic, and most posts exist in both. I am also writing a book, _The Dream That Gave Us Computers_, which follows the old dream of writing down reason itself, from Leibniz to today's language models. Some of its chapters appear here first.
