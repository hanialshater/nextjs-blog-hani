# Org in proportion

One strong agent with the whole task and the whole budget is the baseline to beat. In System 3 Meta's benchmarks it
beat or tied every org design from B5 to B10, and orgs caught up (B11, B12) only once they were in proportion: few warm
members, a shared board, an independent referee and a budget that stopped them. This skill applies P1, P2, P7 and P8
of the city's [PRINCIPLES.md](../../PRINCIPLES.md).

## Designing

1. **Start from one agent (P1).** Write the single-agent design first: one strong agent, the whole task, the whole
   budget. It is a real option, and it is allowed to win.
2. **Name the reason for an org (P1).** Add members only for one or more of these reasons, written down:
   - the work does not fit one context;
   - it runs over a long horizon;
   - it recurs;
   - its risk needs independent checking.
   No reason, no org.
3. **One warm maker per item (P2).** Each item has one maker who keeps its whole context and runs its own checks
   (tests, the referee's fast check, a self-review) inside its own loop. Independent checking happens once, at the
   end, by someone who did not make it. Never more than two makers in a row; a relay of cold stages that each start
   from nothing cost 2.3x and took 2.7x as long, for no gain in accuracy (B6).
4. **Few members, the right models (P7).** At most 4 members in a crew and 8 roles in a corporate design, unless the
   design states a size reason. Use cheaper models where the work allows. Headcount does not buy throughput near the
   ceiling (B13: $128 a point with eight members, against $55 with four in B12).
5. **Money binds before time (P8).** Set a cost target per item: its unit and the amount per item. Write the stop rule:
   work over 2x its target pauses for the owner, and a line of work stops when its gains flatten (for example, two
   rounds each gaining under 1%).
6. **Predictions first.** Say what the design will achieve, and what would show it wrong, before it runs (P10).
7. **The owner approves (P13).** The design, with its reasons, goes to the owner. Objections travel with it.

## Reviewing

For an existing org, ask of each flow and role: does its reason still hold? Compare its cost per item with its target
and with what one agent would have spent. Retire or merge what has no reason; a reason that did not hold is a finding,
recorded with its evidence.

## Checklist

- [ ] The single-agent baseline is written down as an option.
- [ ] Each flow, crew or role states its reason (context, horizon, recurrence, risk).
- [ ] Each item has one maker, who runs its own checks in its own loop.
- [ ] No flow has more than two makers in a row.
- [ ] Independent checks come once, at the end, by someone other than the maker.
- [ ] A crew has 4 members or fewer, a corporate design 8 roles or fewer, or a size reason is stated.
- [ ] Each member's model is chosen for its work, not by default the largest.
- [ ] Each item has a cost target (unit and amount) and a stop rule (over 2x pauses for the owner; flat gains stop).
- [ ] Predictions, with "wrong if", are committed before the first milestone.
- [ ] The owner has approved the design; objections are recorded with it.
- [ ] Any principle the design breaks has its reason in a decision record.
